🔐 Why split a secret? multi-key signatures

Sometimes no one person should hold the whole key. Shamir's Secret Sharing splits a secret into n shares so that any t together can reconstruct it — while t − 1 shares reveal absolutely nothing.

🏛️

Company treasury 3 of 5

Five board members hold one share each. Any three can co-sign to move corporate funds, but no two together can drain the account.

💰

Crypto cold wallet 2 of 3

Split the seed phrase across three vaults in different cities. A fire or theft can destroy one and you still recover the wallet from the other two. One vault alone leaks nothing.

🔑

Password recovery 3 of 5

Give five trusted friends one share each. If you lose your device, any three can help you recover. No friend can spy alone, and even two can't learn a thing.

The design goal. Increase t for security (harder to collude); increase n − t for availability (more shares can be lost without losing the secret).

📐 The geometric idea

t points pin down exactly one polynomial of degree t − 1. Fewer than t leaves infinitely many polynomials that still fit.

t = 2 → a line

1 point · infinitely many lines
2 points · exactly one line

t = 3 → a parabola

2 points · many parabolas
3 points · exactly one

t = 4 → a cubic

3 points · many cubics
4 points · exactly one
The secret is f(0) — where the curve crosses the y-axis. Each share is a point (x, f(x)) for x ≥ 1. Fewer than t of them, the y-intercept is still completely free.

🌀 Why fewer than t shares reveal nothing

Pick any candidate value s for f(0). Together with your t − 1 known shares, that's t points total — which always defines exactly one polynomial of degree t − 1. So every possible secret is consistent with what you hold. No information leaks.

On the graph, the "wiggles" toggle shows this directly: each curve below threshold passes through every selected point and still disagrees about f(0).

Threshold security. With a 3-of-5 scheme, any two colluding officers still face the same guessing problem they had before they talked. Trust is split, not shared.

⚙️ Real Shamir vs. this demo

This demo uses real-number polynomials so you can see the curves. Real deployments use arithmetic in a finite field (all operations mod a large prime), which removes rounding error and makes each coefficient look like random noise.

The geometric intuition is the same: t points define one polynomial of degree t − 1; fewer leave it free.

🔗 Threshold & multi-key signatures

Shamir's scheme is the foundation of threshold signatures and multi-signature wallets: the private signing key is never assembled in one place; instead, any t of n parties can cooperate to sign, and t − 1 can't.
